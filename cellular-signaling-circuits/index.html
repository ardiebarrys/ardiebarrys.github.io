YAP/TAZ as a mechanochemical signal-resolution circuit

Matrix stiffness, attachment and actin tension are read through YAP/TAZ. The framework links signal timing with active termination and recovery of mechanosensitivity.

Central questionCan a cell regain mechanical responsiveness after its force history changes?

NRF2–KEAP1 as a redox signal-resolution circuit

KEAP1 senses redox and electrophilic stress while CUL3–KEAP1 controls NRF2 turnover. KEAP1 renewal, proteostasis, autophagy and reducing capacity decide when the signal ends.

Central questionDoes the redox response resolve once the stress passes, or stay engaged?

CYP1A1 as a conserved metabolic circuit

AhR senses environmental, dietary, microbial and endogenous ligands. Delayed CYP1A1 induction clears susceptible ligands, tying chemical persistence to how long immune and barrier signaling lasts.

The UPR as a proteotoxic stress-resolution circuit

Environmental toxicants act as proteotoxic inputs. IRE1α, PERK and ATF6 decode ER stress into immune outcomes, and failed resolution can shift immune and barrier cells toward persistent dysfunction.

Central questionDoes the response resolve before the next exposure, or stay branch-skewed and persistent?

The connecting question

What determines whether a signal ends?

Activation is only half the story. The other half is how each system changes once the input fades.

01Input history

Force, redox state, ligand supply or pulse pattern.

02Control mechanism

Transport, protein turnover, metabolic feedback or assembly.

03Time course

Latency, persistence, adaptation, termination and recovery.

04Outcome

Responsive baseline, altered cell state or persistent signaling.

A framework, not one mechanism

Shared logic. Distinct biology.

The reviews do not claim every pathway uses the same machinery. They test whether timing, feedback and resolution help organize mechanisms across systems.
